Methodology

How the score is built

The formula, the scaling, and the data behind every PoR score.

The formula

PoR is the average of four component scores, each comparing a hitter's actual outcome to their expected outcome (from Baseball Savant's batted-ball model), scaled against that season's full range of qualified hitters:

PoR = 14 [ MAXBA − (BA − xBA) RANGEBA + MAXSLG − (SLG − xSLG) RANGESLG + MAXwOBA − (wOBA − xwOBA) RANGEwOBA + MAXBABIP − BABIP RANGEBABIP ]

BABIP has no "expected" counterpart, so it's treated differently: league-average BABIP sits close to .300 every season, so a hitter's raw BABIP is scaled directly against that year's min and max — no difference calculation needed.

A high score means the player under-produced relative to their skill (unlucky, likely to progress). A low score means they over-produced (lucky, likely to regress).

Why scale year-by-year

Two scaling approaches were tested: a single fixed scale built from five years of league-wide data, and a scale rebuilt from each season's own min/max. Year-by-year scaling produced meaningfully better accuracy and larger usable sample sizes, at the cost of scores not being directly comparable across seasons (a PoR of 80 in 2022 isn't quite the same as an 80 in 2024). That trade-off was worth it — the metric is currently intended for within-season use, not for comparing a player's score across years.

Why wRC+ is a filter, not an input

An early version tried folding a hitter's prior-year wRC+ directly into the score — boosting PoR for below-average hitters and penalizing it for above-average ones. It made intuitive sense, but it made the metric worse: correlation with next-year wRC+ change dropped from 0.47 to 0.45. Elite hitters near their performance ceiling improve less even when unlucky, simply because they have less room to grow — so wRC+ works better as a filter applied after the score, not a weight baked into it.

Data sources

All batted-ball and expected-stat data comes from Baseball Savant's expected statistics leaderboard; all wRC+ values come from FanGraphs. Analysis covers qualified hitters (min. plate-appearance thresholds noted per test) across the 2021 through 2024 seasons, with each season's PoR score checked against that player's following-season performance.
